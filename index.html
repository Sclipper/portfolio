TaxMate

2024–present

Medium
NestJS, Prisma, PostgreSQL, BullMQ, React 18 with TanStack, Google Document AI, OpenAI, Qdrant, open banking, Stripe
Dimensions
270,000 lines of TypeScript in the API; 149 data models; 445 test files
Role
Founder and CEO. Designed and built the platform.
Visit
taxmate.bg

Bulgarian small businesses still do their books in desktop software from the 2000s and email PDFs to an accountant. TaxMate replaces that with one web app: issue invoices, record expenses, reconcile the bank, and download the VAT return ready for the National Revenue Agency.

The hard part is the expense side. A supplier invoice arrives as a PDF or a phone photo. Google Document AI extracts the supplier, line items, totals and VAT, with an OpenAI fallback for documents it cannot read. Each line is then classified to the company's chart of accounts using embeddings in Qdrant, the supplier is matched or created from its VAT number, and a person reviews the draft before anything is posted. Postings go through configurable journal blueprints, so every document produces correct double-entry bookkeeping.

The output has to satisfy the tax office byte for byte: the sales and purchase journals and the declaration are generated as the exact fixed-width files the NRA portal accepts, in Windows-1251. Around it sit open-banking connections with suggested invoice matches, payroll, recurring invoices, an AI-assisted bulk import, scoped API tokens and Stripe plans with usage limits.

TaxMate for AI assistants

2026

Medium
Model Context Protocol SDK, oidc-provider, PostgreSQL, Express, Zod
Dimensions
Access tokens live 300 seconds; scopes are read or write, per company
Role
Designed and built

Letting Claude or ChatGPT work inside someone's accounts is mostly a security problem. TaxMate runs its own OAuth and OpenID Connect issuer next to a Streamable HTTP MCP server. At consent the user picks which companies the assistant may see and whether it may write; each choice becomes a company-scoped API token, sealed at rest with AES-GCM.

Assistants receive ES256 access tokens bound to the MCP resource and valid for five minutes. Every call is checked against the live grant, so revoking access takes effect immediately, and refresh tokens rotate and cannot be reused or widened. The MCP server talks to TaxMate only through the same public API customers use. There is no back door.

Hyperion

2025–present

Medium
NestJS, BullMQ, PostgreSQL, Puppeteer, OpenAI and the Vercel AI SDK, MCP
Dimensions
More than 40 services and research repositories
Role
Technical lead, through Delta Forge

Lent by a quantitative hedge fund. Code withheld at the client's request.

A small fund wanted its analysts' reading done by software. I built a research ingestion service that signs in to institutional macro research providers with browser automation, collects reports, model-portfolio allocations and trading signals, and uses LLMs to turn unstructured PDFs into rows in PostgreSQL. Each provider is a module, so adding a source does not touch the rest.

On top of that data sits an agentic trading system. LLM agents call tools that combine the macro research with market-regime analysis and technical indicators, and return trade signals with take-profit and stop-loss levels. A scheduled scanner runs across assets and timeframes; strategy, position-management and broker code sit behind tests, timeouts and safe fallbacks, and the tools are exposed over MCP. Around it: order routing, a ledger and reconciliation, backtesting, and an assistant in the team's Slack that answers in plain language.

Намери фотограф

“Find a photographer”

2026

Medium
Next.js 15 and React 19 on a NestJS engine; Prisma, PostgreSQL, Stripe, Resend, Playwright
Dimensions
An engine of 21 modules, from dispatch to session bookings
Role
Built through Delta Forge, product to production
Visit
nameri-fotograf.bg

A client describes a shoot once, compares portfolios and offers, and exchanges contact details with the photographer they choose. The site is the gallery: photographs lead and prices stay quiet.

Behind it is nameri-engine, a NestJS service I wrote to run a “find a…” marketplace: requests, dispatch to matching providers, offers, contact unlocks, bookings, reviews and email. It is built so the next vertical is a thin front end over it; photography is the first. When photographers turned down commission on their jobs, the first revenue became a client-paid booking fee: a held hour opens Stripe Checkout and is released only after Stripe confirms the session closed unpaid.

“Find an accountant”

2026

Medium
Next.js 15, Tailwind 4, Prisma, Supabase, the Vercel AI SDK with Claude, Resend
Dimensions
A 13-field brief, about 60 seconds to fill
Role
Designed and built
Visit
nameri-schetovoditel.bg

Choosing an accountant in Bulgaria means phoning around. Here a business owner fills in one short brief, a server-side model call turns it into a clear summary for accountants, and staff hand-pick who receives it. Manual curation is the product, not a stopgap.

The client gets up to three proposals and ranks them with their own weights across price, speed, experience, software, communication and industry, with the score breakdown shown rather than hidden. Success fees are invoiced through TaxMate's own public API.

Намери чистач

“Find a cleaner”

2026

Medium
Next.js 15, React 19, Tailwind 4, Prisma, PostgreSQL, Zod, Playwright
Dimensions
4 cities; one private link per person per job
Role
Built through Delta Forge
Visit
nameri-chistach.bg

Demand first: a person describes their flat once, cleaners who work in that district send a price for that specific job, and the person compares the offers side by side. Afterwards both sides rate each other.

Nobody needs an account. Every link is a single-purpose credential: only its digest is stored, it opens exactly one thing, and an unknown, wrong or malformed token gets the same unavailable screen, so nothing can be enumerated. The money path is covered end to end in a real browser.

Fetchpaw

2026

Medium
Chrome and Edge extension (Manifest V3), TypeScript, pdf.js, Playwright; a Node and PostgreSQL server with Stripe
Dimensions
10,600 lines of TypeScript in the extension, 1,700 in its server; 5 fake portals in the end-to-end suite
Role
Built through Delta Forge
Visit
fetchpaw.app

Every month someone has to log in to Vercel, AWS, Google Ads and a dozen other billing pages and download the invoices for the accountant. Fetchpaw does it inside the browser you are already signed in to: pick a month, press Fetch, and it visits each supplier in a background tab, saves that month's invoices with a summary sheet, and, given a bank statement, lists every payment that has no invoice.

It holds no passwords and has no per-site scrapers. A browser-agent model picks each click from the page, and once it reaches the invoice list that page is remembered, so next month goes straight there with no model call. When the model is unsure, or an option reads like paying, cancelling or changing a plan, the run stops and asks. The end-to-end suite drives the built extension in Chromium against fake portals, a Bulgarian telecom with icon-only buttons among them, and checks the exact files written to disk.

opencal-sync

2026

Medium
Next.js, SQLite, Composio, Docker
Dimensions
Polls every 5 minutes; about 8,640 API calls a month per calendar
Licence
MIT. Source on GitHub

A free, self-hosted alternative to paid calendar-sync services. It mirrors events between Google and Outlook accounts as private “Busy” blockers or full clones, one way or both, and publishes a page of your merged free slots.

Google supports incremental sync; Outlook, through Composio, does not, so each poll fetches the window and diffs it against the last known mappings to infer deletions. A local mapping table makes every write idempotent and stops sync loops. Events marked free never create blockers, and attendees are never copied, so nobody gets re-invited.
